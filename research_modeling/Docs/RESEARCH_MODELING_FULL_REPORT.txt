===================================================================================================================
01. RESEARCH OBJECTIVE AND SCOPE
===================================================================================================================
The research task is to classify natural-language software requirements into functional requirements (FR) or
non-functional requirements (NFR). The classical ML phase establishes transparent, computationally accessible
benchmarks for future comparisons with neural networks and pretrained transformer models.

Primary performance measures:
 - NFR precision: correct NFR predictions divided by all predicted NFR examples.
 - NFR recall: correctly detected NFR examples divided by all actual NFR examples.
 - NFR F1: harmonic mean of NFR precision and NFR recall.
 - Macro-F1: arithmetic mean of class-wise FR and NFR F1 scores.
 - Balanced accuracy: mean recall across FR and NFR.
 - Accuracy: correct classifications out of all examples.
 - Average precision and ROC-AUC: threshold-independent measures of score ordering.
 - False negatives (FN): actual NFR predicted FR; of special interest because they are missed NFRs.
 - False positives (FP): actual FR predicted NFR.

The study did not aim to assert project-independent generalization, independent manual label verification,
or statistically confirm a winner after unrestricted model searches. It aimed to establish and document
reproducible baseline evidence while preserving an untouched test partition for final evaluation.

===================================================================================================================
02. FROZEN DATASET, PROVENANCE AND CONSTRAINTS
===================================================================================================================
The D2 subset was selected during earlier dataset exploration from a unified multi-source collection. The broader
collection contained 8,854 raw requirements from D1–D5. D2 was preferred for a controlled binary FR/NFR task.
The original D2 source included 4,993 rows, with 4,963 original FR/NFR labeled records. Prior screening reduced
this to 4,835 source-consistent v1.0 candidates and then 4,833 v1.1 candidates after quarantining two records.
The version 1.1 grouping update preserved the original grouping relations while merging two additional high-
similarity connections. Final version 1.1 contained 4,625 similarity groups.

The frozen split contains:
  PARTITION          FR         NFR        TOTAL         FRACTION
  train            2,224      1,154       3,378         69.8945%
  validation         489        239         728         15.0631%
  test               488        239         727         15.0424%
  TOTAL            3,201      1,632       4,833        100.0000%

Quality controls before modeling:
 - No duplicated normalized text keys among the final 4,833 candidate rows.
 - Original quarantined records did not re-enter the data.
 - No similarity group was split across train/validation/test.
 - The manifest SHA-256 was checked in modeling scripts against saved split metadata.
 - An exhaustive audit compared 5,444,246 cross-partition text pairs under character 3–5 gram TF-IDF cosine.
 - There were 0 pairs at or above the predeclared review similarity threshold 0.85.
 - There were 17 cross-partition pairs at or above 0.80 but below 0.85.
 - The maximum observed cross-partition similarity was 0.848999 (train vs validation).
 - Dataset technical readiness passed with limitations in Script 56.

What these facts do NOT prove:
 - The labels are not established as independently human-verified.
 - The source project identities cannot support a valid claim of original-project independence.
 - A lexical threshold does not guarantee the absence of paraphrases or semantic duplicates.
 - Some AI-assisted annotation decisions are provisional.
 - Similarity groups are constructed isolation units, not automatically independent software projects.

The dataset was frozen as D2-v1.1 before the modeling experiments summarized below. The previous D2-v1.0
partition and its historical review evidence were left unchanged.

===================================================================================================================
03. EXPERIMENT-WIDE PROTOCOL
===================================================================================================================
- Random seed: 42 (where applicable).
- Five-fold StratifiedGroupKFold with shuffle=True and seed 42 for training-only cross-validation.
- Fold grouping key: similarity_group. All members of a group stayed within a single fold partition.
- Each cross-validation fold fitted its TF-IDF vocabulary, IDF statistics, and any SVD components on that fold's
  training examples only. Held-out fold text was transformed without refitting.
- Full-model fitting used 3,378 train requirements. Evaluation used the 728 validation requirements.
- The 727 test examples were not used for classifier predictions, threshold searches, or model selection.
- For binary scores, FR=0 and NFR=1. Confusion matrices are [[actual FR predicted FR, actual FR predicted NFR],
  [actual NFR predicted FR, actual NFR predicted NFR]], conventionally [[TN, FP], [FN, TP]].
- Most models used their default prediction rule, logistic threshold 0.50 or SVM decision score boundary zero.
- Threshold studies in Scripts 59–61 were development-only and did not use the test set.
- Validation was reused for multiple comparisons. Final validation rankings therefore have selection optimism.
- Five folds from the same source collection do not constitute external replicated datasets.
- Each experiment saved its output under research_modeling/outputs/{tables,reports}/.

Standard word TF-IDF when applicable:
  analyzer='word'; ngram_range=(1,2); min_df=2; max_df=0.95;
  sublinear_tf=True; lowercase=True; strip_accents='unicode';
  max_features=100000; norm='l2'.
  Full-train feature vocabulary in the original Script 57: 7,787 features.

Other representations:
  Character TF-IDF: analyzer='char_wb', ngram_range=(3,5), min_df=2,
  max_df=0.95, sublinear_tf=True, max_features=100000, norm='l2'.
  Full-train character feature vocabulary in Script 66: 19,261 features.
  Combined representation: horizontally concatenated word + char_wb TF-IDF, then L2 normalization.
  Histogram gradient boosting: word TF-IDF plus 100-component TruncatedSVD fitted on training-only folds.

   better score ranking.

F. Linear models proved particularly competitive with sparse natural-language TF-IDF features. Tested tree

   labeling, and text patterns could be a target of explicitly qualitative later analysis, without using them

21. LEAKAGE PROTECTION, THREATS TO VALIDITY AND UNRESOLVED ITEMS
===================================================================================================================
PROTECTIONS RECORDED:
 - Frozen D2-v1.1 source and partition assignments.
 - No reused similarity groups across train/validation/test partitions.
 - No cross-partition pairs >=0.85 in a specified exhaustive lexical similarity audit.
 - Training-fold feature fitting rather than refitting on held-out fold data.
 - SVD fitted inside fold-training data for boosting pipeline.
 - Threshold searches restricted to training OOF and/or development validation; test untouched.
 - Repeated SHA-256 input integrity checks against the frozen manifest.
 - Explicitly separate classical modeling folder and new deep-learning folder.

THREATS AND CAVEATS:
 1. Repeated model comparison on the same validation set can lead to overfitting model choices to that set.
 2. The strongest validation model may not remain strongest on untouched test data.
 3. Source labels were not independently human-reviewed and some AI-assisted correction rules are provisional.
 4. No original-project-independent split can be confidently claimed from current provenance.
 5. Lexical similarity at a 0.85 threshold is not a definitive semantic near-duplicate test.
 6. The 17 pairs at or above cosine 0.80 remain below the review threshold and are not automatically harmless.
 7. NFR examples are fewer than FR (class imbalance), motivating minority-class metrics.
 8. Paired bootstrap CIs on reused validation do not remove model-selection bias.
 9. Reported unadjusted McNemar p-values are exploratory and test overall correctness, not F1 directly.
10. Hyperparameters are fixed or lightly explored rather than exhaustively optimized.
11. Fold-level mean results do not estimate cross-project robustness.
12. RBF SVC decision scores, LinearSVC margins and SGD scores are not calibrated probabilities.
13. Combined-feature pipelines and SVD pipelines change both representation and potentially capacity.
14. No final sealed test metrics exist at the end of this archive.
15. No transformer or deep neural result was generated within this recorded classical ML phase.

RECOMMENDED COMPARISON PRACTICE IN THE NEW PHASE:
 - Use the identical D2-v1.1 frozen manifest as the data source, not a rebuilt split.
 - Keep the 727 test requirements sealed until an explicitly documented final evaluation.
 - Keep numerical class mapping FR=0, NFR=1 consistent.
 - Preserve training-only fitting rules and similarity group constraints where applicable.
 - Use validation for development but report the extensive historical validation reuse.
 - Record pretrained checkpoint, tokenizer, max length, truncation, learning rate, batch size,
   effective batch size, epochs, seeds, checkpoint choice and hardware for neural experiments.
 - Compare new neural models against at least S02 and a transparent conventional reference, with the
   same evaluation labels and metrics. Report resource costs where possible.
 - Avoid claiming external project generalization without a suitable independently sourced test collection.
