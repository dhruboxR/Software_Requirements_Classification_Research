01. RESEARCH OBJECTIVE AND OUTCOME
----------------------------------
The research investigated whether a unified software requirements dataset could support a credible classification benchmark, with particular emphasis on fair evaluation of minority classes and avoiding duplicate/near-duplicate leakage. The work follows an earlier, separate PROMISE+ experiment program, which ended after Phase 8; those earlier model findings are historical context and were NOT imported as model decisions for this fresh dataset.

The original ambition was to examine the full unified dataset and its component sources before choosing a defensible training corpus. The choice was NOT made merely on raw size or headline accuracy. The criteria included source traceability, class taxonomy, annotation agreement, duplicates, repeated text across sources, contradictory labels, original project provenance, near-duplicate relationships, sufficient independent-like minority support, and the feasibility of frozen grouped partitions.

FINAL SELECTION:
- Primary candidate: D2, binary FR versus NFR.
- Final candidate protocol: D2-v1.1.
- Final candidate size: 4,833 unique normalized requirement texts.
- Class counts: FR 3,201; NFR 1,632.
- Frozen partitions: train 3,378; validation 728; test 727.
- Frozen test NFR support: 239.
- Similarity components in final candidate: 4,625.
- Exhaustive lexical cross-partition audit: 5,444,246 pairwise comparisons; zero pairs at or above cosine 0.85 under the chosen character n-gram TF-IDF representation.
- Technical readiness: PASS.
- Scientific approval: CONDITIONAL / ready with documented limitations, NOT independent human-label validation and NOT a verified unseen-project benchmark.
- Model training and test evaluation at time of archival record: NOT STARTED.

- Rows affected by those global conflicting-text groups: 363.
- Of the 162 globally conflicting groups: 130 are cross-source; 32 are within a single source.

Normalization used for text keys during major audits: Unicode NFKC normalization, whitespace collapsing, leading/trailing whitespace removal, and casefolding. These procedures detect normalized exact text matches; they do not equate all semantic paraphrases.

Important interpretation: Different label schemes coexist in the unified dataset. A cross-source 'label conflict' can reflect incompatible taxonomies, annotation disagreement, or both. The count does not by itself imply that every affected row is a human labeling mistake. Consequently, source-specific taxonomy analysis was necessary before merging or relabeling anything.

20. THESIS-READY METHODOLOGY SUMMARY
-----------------------------------
The study initially examined a unified multi-source software requirements corpus containing 8,854 records from five constituent sources (D1-D5). Source-wise checks assessed text overlap with original data, annotation taxonomy, duplicate and near-duplicate requirements, contradictory labels, project metadata reliability, and minority-class evaluation feasibility. The data exhibited substantial cross-source repetition and incompatible or inconsistent annotations, so the constituent sources were evaluated separately. D3 offered partially recoverable original-project metadata but yielded extremely small test samples for several of its eleven labels under grouped-split simulations. D4 showed repeated generated requirements across language models and rounds and limited independent-like NFR support. D5 was too small and substantially overlapped other sources. D2 had the strongest support for a binary functional versus non-functional requirements task, although its project identifiers could not support a defensible independent-project holdout.

For D2, conflicts and previously flagged near-duplicate disagreements were provisionally excluded, normalized exact texts were deduplicated, and retained labels were checked against the immediate original FR/NFR workbook. This produced 4,835 source-consistent candidate texts, with 36 additional source-label cases withheld. A frozen initial split was created using connected groups from candidate lexical similarities. A subsequent full cross-partition text-similarity audit identified three overlooked pairs, including a nearly identical FR/NFR labeling contradiction. These cases were assessed through documented AI-assisted provisional judgments: the conflicting pair was quarantined without relabeling, and two related/equivalent same-label pairs were connected for grouping. The resulting D2-v1.1 candidate contained 4,833 texts (3,201 FR; 1,632 NFR) in 4,625 connected groups.

A reproducible grouped split with seed 42 allocated 3,378 texts to training (2,224 FR; 1,154 NFR), 728 to validation (489 FR; 239 NFR), and 727 to testing (488 FR; 239 NFR). An exhaustive 3-5-character TF-IDF cosine audit evaluated 5,444,246 cross-partition pairs and found zero at the predefined 0.85 similarity review threshold, while 17 exceeded 0.80. The final frozen D2-v1.1 protocol passed the available technical integrity checks and was designated ready for controlled FR/NFR modeling with documented limitations. No independent human annotation verification was performed, no verified unseen-project generalization claim is supported, and neither model training nor test evaluation had begun at the time of this archive.
